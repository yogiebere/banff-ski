How runs are rated

Two ratings (the user, 2026-11-15: "Go ahead and do that style for the banffs as well"). First, Sunshine's own: its lifts and trails report, read through the conditions tracker (Easiest, More Difficult, Most Difficult and Experts Only; Delirium Dive and Wild West, its extreme terrain, count as extreme). Then, in brackets where it differs, the run's level on the Centre scale, measured as below. SRG Skiing's calls (a run that's really a double, or needs mandatory air) replace the measured level.

Level: the steepest 500 ft (150 m) of each run from OpenSkiMap's elevation profile (where OpenStreetMap draws a run as several lines, its easiest complete way down, which the run card's chart shows), marked down by up to 2° when that pitch sits far above the average of the run's main section (flat lead-ins and run-outs left out), then cut on the Centre scale: each colour centred on its typical run, the higher of the Colorado top-10 and Copper medians (green 13.2°, blue 17.5°, black 25.5°, double 31.2°; extreme 41°, from Sunshine's Delirium Dive and Wild West), with half-steps halfway between. Bands: green under 14.3°, green/blue to 16.4°, blue to 19.5°, blue/black to 23.5°, black to 26.9°, black/double to 29.8°, double to 33.6°, double/extreme (◆◆/EX) to 38.5°, extreme above. Ungroomed snow adds 2° to a black or double that's groomed on fewer than 30% of days; greens, blues and blacks groomed more often than that count as groomed (the user's rule, 2026-11-15). A powder day removes the adder. Thin early-season cover adds up to 3° more where rocks and features need filling in, from SRG Skiing, the research, cliffy ground near the line, steep ungroomed pitch, and trail-map blacks and doubles that measure easier. The season goes by mid-mountain settled base, not the date: early under 100 cm, mid-season 100 to 150 cm (half the adder), late season 150 cm and up (none). On Jan 1 2026 both resorts were already past 150 cm. Where SRG Skiing says a run is really a double black, or needs mandatory air (EX), his call wins.

Stars: a baseline from SRG Skiing's guide and wider research, shifted by each run's exposure, tree cover, grooming, powder and firm-snow behaviour, then ranked within its level. So five stars means best in its level today, not hardest.
