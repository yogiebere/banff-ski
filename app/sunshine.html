How runs are rated

Level: the steepest 150 m of each run from OpenSkiMap's elevation profile (where OpenStreetMap draws a run as several lines, its easiest complete way down, which the run card's chart shows), marked down by up to 2° when that pitch sits far above the average of the run's main section (flat lead-ins and run-outs left out), then cut on the Centre scale: each colour centred on its typical run, the higher of the Colorado top-10 and Copper medians (green 13°, blue 17.3°, black 24°, double 31.2°), with half-steps halfway between. Bands: green under 14.1°, green/blue to 16.2°, blue to 19.0°, blue/black to 22.3°, black to 25.8°, black/double to 29.4°, double above. Ungroomed snow adds 2° and rotational grooming 1°. A powder day removes the adder. Thin early-season cover adds up to 3° more where rocks and features need filling in, from SRG Skiing, the research, cliffy ground near the line, steep ungroomed pitch, and trail-map blacks and doubles that measure easier. The season goes by mid-mountain settled base, not the date: early under 100 cm, mid-season 100 to 150 cm (half the adder), late season 150 cm and up (none). On Jan 1 2026 both resorts were already past 150 cm. Where SRG Skiing says a run is really a double black, or needs mandatory air (EX), his call wins.

Stars: a baseline from SRG Skiing's guide and wider research, shifted by each run's exposure, tree cover, grooming, powder and firm-snow behaviour, then ranked within its level. So five stars means best in its level today, not hardest.
