How runs are rated

Primary source: SkiTnB (youtube.com/@SkiTnB), the user's call on 2026-11-14. His ◆ and ◆◆ calls come from each Crystal video's run list and highlight badges, and his star picks from his favourite and hardest runs and his comments. Where his call and the measured level are a full level apart, his call wins. His ◆ and ◆◆ have no half steps, so half a level apart they agree and the measurement stays. On the Centre scale Blazing Elk and West Face measure double, a full level above his ◆, so they show his black. Roads and traverses keep their own grade. Lines drawn on his labelled photos of the faces (his Google Drive inset maps) were fitted to the terrain and added or redrawn; each such run card says so. Runs he doesn't cover fall back to The Unofficial Guide to Crystal Mountain (crystal-guide.com, 2015–16): its 1–10 scores set their stars, its difficulty replaces the measured level where it's a level or more from Crystal's colour (Memorial Forest double black, Melby's black), and its Low Coverage, Rocks and Cliffs tags count toward the thin-cover adder. Lines either source rates that have no line on the map are listed under "Off-map lines".

Two ratings. The symbol first is Crystal's own: its 2026-27 trail map and Northway inset, or its lift and trail report for runs the map doesn't mark. Where the two disagree (Melby's, Sheppard, Berry Patch) the trail map wins and the run card notes the report. Local names inside terrain Crystal rates as a whole (Southback, High Campbell) take that zone's double black. Jeremy's Traverse, which the user added, carries the user's blue. Brackets alone mean Crystal doesn't rate the run.

In brackets, where it differs: the Centre scale. The user's scale (2026-11-15), used on every map: each colour centred on its typical run, the higher of the Colorado top-10 and Copper medians (green 13.2°, blue 17.5°, black 25.5°, double 31.2°; extreme 41°, from Sunshine's Delirium Dive and Wild West), with half-steps halfway between. Bands: green under 14.3°, green/blue to 16.4°, blue to 19.5°, blue/black to 23.5°, black to 26.9°, black/double to 29.8°, double to 33.6°, double/extreme (◆◆/EX) to 38.5°, extreme above. Roads and cat tracks are left out when the scale is set, since resorts colour them by where they lead. The score: The steepest 150 m of each run's elevation profile (where OpenStreetMap draws a run as several lines, its easiest complete way down, which the run card's chart shows), marked down by up to 2° when that pitch sits far above the average of the run's main section (flat lead-ins and run-outs left out, unless that leaves less than 150 m), then cut on those bands. Only downhill stretches count. A run shorter than 150 m is measured over its whole length (Frisco, Last Scream, Ms. Magoo and about 20 others). Roads, cat tracks and traverses, found by name or by a gentle line across a steep slope, are rated by their own grade: the steepest 300 m with no trim, because OSM draws their switchbacks and corners as straight drops (Jeremy's Road's line turns straight down a 28° slope at its end). Area runs (Bear Pits, Snorting Elk Bowl, Northway Bowl and others) are rated by the steepest 150 m of fall line inside them on USGS bare-earth lidar. Ungroomed snow adds 2° to a black or double that's groomed on fewer than 30% of days, from archived daily grooming reports (2026-11-15 research); greens, blues and blacks groomed more often than that count as groomed (the user's rule). A powder day removes the adder. Thin early-season cover adds up to 3° more where rocks and features need filling in, from SkiTnB, the guide and other research, cliffy ground near the line, steep ungroomed pitch, and Crystal blacks and doubles that measure easier. The season goes by mid-mountain settled base, not the date: early under 100 cm, mid-season 100 to 150 cm (half the adder), late season 150 cm and up (none). Enter the mid-mountain base from Crystal's snow report, or the average of its base and summit depths if it lists only those.

Stars are the run's own, in halves (the user's call, 2026-11-15): on a bluebird packed-powder day, half the guide's 1–10 score, so Lumpy Lane's 2/10 is one star and Tinkerbell's 5/10 two and a half; SkiTnB's pick where he rates the line; else other research (Ski Magazine, zrankings, trip reports and five archived grooming reports). Other conditions move a run up or down by how much they help or hurt it: exposure, tree cover, grooming, and powder and firm-snow behaviour. Runs no source covers, mostly in Northway, start at three stars.

Line colours, the level chips and the stars follow Crystal's rating. The Southback is hike-to from the top of Chair 6, and Northway's runs start at numbered gates. These, Chair 6 and the Throne usually open late after storms. A Hike-to or Gated tag on a run card says how you get in, where the research says so. Pitch comes from OpenSkiMap's elevation profiles, which match USGS bare-earth lidar to under a metre, or from that lidar for lines OSM lacks; Copernicus GLO-30 is only the shaded terrain.
